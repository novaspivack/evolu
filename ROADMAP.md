# Evolu roadmap

Set by Nova Spivack; sequenced so that the world is not asked to hold everything at once. Last updated with Generation 17 (22 November 2026).

## How this file is used

- There are two copies of the roadmap. This file is for people. The other is `K.SEED` in `src/maker.js`: the same items, in the same order, in the maker's notebook, where the maker reads them during a run with the instruction to **work on NOW, prepare NEXT, and let the rest wait**. Change one and change the other.
- An item moves up a stage when a real run shows the stage before it working, not when its code exists.
- Built means the mechanics exist and pass the tests. It does not mean any being has used them. What real minds have actually done is in [`docs/design-log.md`](docs/design-log.md).

## The rule for everything

Open and emergent. Make things possible, give them a cost and a use, and never script an outcome. Everything acts through one shared language of effects on attributes, so plants, animals, tools, rituals, places and the maker's interventions can all be combined with each other and the results can be learned, taught and passed on. Beings are told only what they could perceive.

Knowledge is earned slowly, in ages. Later things need earlier ones, need materials that must be found and carried, and need people enough to make them.

## THE NEXT BIG ROUND: the trees, and what is built from them (Nova, 23 November)

The purpose: that progress along the trees really happens, that the trees work together, and that it can be seen. The sim should be able to go all the way to advanced societies.

**First an audit, with numbers from the runs, before anything is changed.** Done on 23 November: `docs/tree-audit.md`.

- *What there is.* 68 ways in four trees (craft 31, mind 18, society 9, life 10) over six ages. A way comes within reach by practice (a count of something done) and by the ways it needs, and opens at a deep reflection; hardship brings some nearer. It then passes by teaching and by being watched.
- *What to find out.* How far each run got and how fast; which ways were never reached and why (the practice never done, a needed way missing, never offered); whether a band advances together or one being runs ahead; whether a way once found changes what its holder does; which ways have nothing behind them (from about the fourth age the ways, minerals and buildings have names and looks and little that they do); how the four trees lean on one another, and where they do not but should.
- *The same for the other systems that touch them:* recipes, tools and designs, buildings, minerals and goods, standing and roles, culture and teaching, the maker's heritage.

**Then, from what the audit shows:**

- Progress that works for a band and a settlement, not only for one being (the near-term item above is its first step).
- **Buildings and other structures that change to the eye as the ages pass,** and more kinds in the library where a line of descent has gaps.
- **The maker designs structures from building blocks,** each block with its attributes (what it shelters, holds, yields, teaches, defends, what it costs and needs), in step with the trees, when needed or wanted. ## LATER: ages three and four

## SCALE: from individuals to groups, to peoples, to planets (Nova, 23 November; farther out)

**The problem.** Claude allows a page only so many thoughts in a given time, whatever the number of beings. At forty-five alive the clock of the world already runs at a third to under a half of its pace. Hundreds cannot each have a mind, let alone thousands or millions.

**Nova's direction.** Past some population the sim deals in groups: the statistical life of a group, and the thoughts, dealings, culture and story of the group, in place of each individual. Later the same step again: a planet and its species dealing with another planet, with other species on it; then several star systems in a galaxy, where species (AI and cyborgs among them) contend. Each is a change of mode. Design for it now.

**How I would build it (Claude's view, for Nova to accept or change).**

- *Resolution by attention, not one switch for the whole world.* The number of minds stays fixed (thirty to forty), whatever the population. They are given to those who matter to the story: leaders, the notable, whoever the watcher looks at. Everyone else lives as a member of a group (a household, a band, a settlement) that runs on the world's laws in aggregate, with one mind for the group that thinks about the group's needs, quarrels and plans. A member becomes an individual when attention falls on them and folds back when it leaves, with what happened to them kept.
- *The two grains must agree.* The rates a group runs on (births, deaths, food, learning, quarrels) are measured from worlds run as individuals, so that a settlement lived as a group comes out like the same settlement lived person by person. That is a test that can be written.
- *The same step repeats.* Group to people or nation, to planet and species, to star system: each time the unit that thinks grows and what is below it becomes numbers. If the first step is built as a general one (a thing that has members, state, knowledge, culture and a mind), the later ones are the same thing again with larger units.
- *What it must not lose.* What is interesting in Evolu is that beings have inner lives. Faces are kept: any member can be called up as a person, drawn from what the group is, and stays that person from then on.
- *First steps that are useful now.* The settlement as a holder of knowledge and stores (the near-term item) is already a group with state. A chronicle of each band and settlement. The save written so that a world has levels.
- *When.* Not before the trees work. A reasonable trigger is about sixty alive, where the clock is at a third.

## Known open problems

- **Starvation in winter.** Ten of seventeen over 41 days. Generation 15 brought storage, but on the eve of famine; Generation 16 answers with foresight in autumn, a land that no longer runs down, a milder winter and bodies that reach their stores.
- **Peoples too small and too closely related to grow.** Generation 16 answered with newcomers, twins and longer lives, and the world went from six to twelve; Generation 17 adds women and men, which narrows who can pair, and bands of four to offset it.
- **A carried-on world cannot grow at its edges.** Only a new world has the veiled rim. Now in NEXT.
- **Nothing in Generation 20 has been balanced by a run:** new hunters (wolves, bears, snakes, shadowcats, rivermaws), new food, three new peoples.
- **Why prompts were not cut further (the measuring that led to fewer thoughts in Generation 25):** the prompt was measured twice after Generation 24 and neither half offers a large cut. Of a routine prompt of 29,200 characters (days 26 to 60 of the fourteenth run), about 9,600 are the same at every thought and about 19,500 are the being's own situation: itself 6,500, what it perceives 3,000, its past and people 2,900, the acts open to it 2,700, what just happened 2,300. The state is already relative to the being (what it sees within 11 steps) and already recent. Splitting the standing text into files a mind reads on request would save 3,000 to 4,500 characters; Nova judged that not worth the tradeoff. What the service refuses is calls too often, so the lever for a large population is the number of calls: every being thinks on a fixed clock, about every 90 moments, whether or not anything weighty happened. First measure, from the run files, how many thoughts changed nothing; then let a being think sooner when something weighty happens and later when only routine things have. One call for several beings standing together is the larger saving and the larger change; consider it after.
- **Prompts and refusals:** middle 30,700 characters and longest 45,600 in the fourteenth run, with forty-three alive. Refusals began at this size. Generation 23 changed the pace only, so that the next run shows what the pace alone does; cut the prompt next.
- **Prompts grew through Generation 21 too:** median 29,000 characters and longest 38,000 in the twelfth run (24,000 and 36,800 in the eighth). No call failed and a thought took 7 seconds, so nothing was cut in Generation 21 beyond the list of plants; if thoughts slow or fail, this is the first place to look.
- **Not yet used by real minds in twelve days on the globe:** fishing, the canoe, kayak, sled and igloo; one animal tamed. Too soon to call a fault.
- **A band of one people that settles in another's climate** (Wood-folk on the ice) pays for it heavily in winter. The pull of the homeland did not bring them back. Watch before changing anything.
- **A thought took 10 seconds in the eleventh run,** three times what it took in the tenth. Unexplained; it sets how often a being can think.
- **Acts that never reached a body.** `claim` and `venture` were unreachable for real minds in Generations 16 and 17 because tests called the body directly. Fixed in 18, with a check that reads the acts out of the prompt.
- **Minds hunt very little:** 2 plan steps of 1,274 in the tenth run. Generation 18 points the hungry at prey; unknown whether they will go.
- **Speed.** A world moment costs about 2.8 ms in the larger world, up from 2.
- **Nothing passes between strangers.** In seven runs: no trade, no planned fight, no animal tamed, and peoples that meet a handful of times.
- **No tale, song, myth or teaching has yet been made** by a real mind, though sayings and hard lessons spread and cross between peoples.
- **Asked for on 23 November and not yet built** (in the order I would take them):
  1. Vehicles and craft that move (a cart on a road, a train between stations, a ship between harbours, a craft of the air between fields), and goods carried between settlements as a thing in the world (caravans, sea lanes).
  2. The troubles of later ages beyond smoke: crime, garbage, poverty, corruption, social unrest, political division, wars between settlements of one people; then booms, crashes and recessions, technology bubbles, gilded ages; then forms of rule (dictatorship, oligarchy) and terrorism.
  3. Culture that turns with the age: old stories kept in modernised form, tales becoming histories, science beside and then in place of myth in what is written, not only in what presses.
  4. An aphrodisiac and plants that change a trait for good (each needs a new kind of effect).
  5. Islands in the near ocean; seas and the unknown for a world carried on.
  6. A newborn kept near its parents (one was gored at a day old in the eighteenth run).
  7. Groups (SCALE): bands and peoples thinking as one when there are too many to think alone; then the planet, its species, and the worlds of other stars as places of play. The view beyond the planet is drawn (Generation 31) and nothing in it is simulated.
  8. The far end of the tree: superintelligence with its goods and harms, nanotech, biowar, mass unemployment, robots and conflict with them, contact with others.
- **Prompts are at a middle of 34,900 characters** (eighteenth run), with a longest of 48,700. About 13,300 is standing text and the reply format; the ways a being knows take 4,900. To be cut by section, measured first.
- **A newborn can wander off and be killed** (a day-old child was gored in the eighteenth run). Not looked into.
- **No offering, no trade and no forge** in the eighteenth run.
- **Founders age out together.** In the seventeenth run nine died of age in ten days and two were born. Generation 27 answers with courting that is answered and with the maker's longer life reaching the living; the ages of founders were left as they are. If births still do not keep pace, spread the founders' ages.
- **The chronicler's prompt is about 100,000 characters,** a quarter of it the numbers sent twice (now and at the last chapter).
- **Almost all water in a world made before Generation 27 can be swum,** so a vessel matters little there. Only new worlds have seas.
- **The store is a quarter full:** 24 MB of 100 MB and 569 documents on 23 November, nearly all of it the chunks of past runs. Old runs will have to be pruned or the record thinned before it fills.
- **The record of a run is too large to read from the store.** An interrupted run can be read only through its final state and, from Generation 15, the maker's own small document. A full analysis needs the downloaded run log.
- **The later ages are sketches.** From about the fourth age the ways, minerals and buildings have names and looks but little that they do.
- **Prompts are long and growing:** a middle of 24,000 characters and a longest of 36,800 in the eighth run.
- **The maker's report that recipe counts did not update** in the sixth run: no fault found.
