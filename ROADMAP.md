# Evolu roadmap

Set by Nova Spivack; sequenced so that the world is not asked to hold everything at once. Last updated with Generation 17 (22 November 2026).

## How this file is used

- There are two copies of the roadmap. This file is for people. The other is `K.SEED` in `src/maker.js`: the same items, in the same order, in the maker's notebook, where the maker reads them during a run with the instruction to **work on NOW, prepare NEXT, and let the rest wait**. Change one and change the other.
- An item moves up a stage when a real run shows the stage before it working, not when its code exists.
- Built means the mechanics exist and pass the tests. It does not mean any being has used them. What real minds have actually done is in [`docs/design-log.md`](docs/design-log.md).

## The rule for everything

Open and emergent. Make things possible, give them a cost and a use, and never script an outcome. Everything acts through one shared language of effects on attributes, so plants, animals, tools, rituals, places and the maker's interventions can all be combined with each other and the results can be learned, taught and passed on. Beings are told only what they could perceive.

Knowledge is earned slowly, in ages. Later things need earlier ones, need materials that must be found and carried, and need people enough to make them.

## NEXT: once NOW is seen working

**From Nova's notes of 22 November, evening, after the globe was built:**

- **Local time** (Nova's second mode: run only what is in view and pause the rest). Not built, and advised against: the world itself costs about 2 ms a moment, so pausing it saves nothing that can be felt; what is scarce is thoughts, and Generation 20 lets the world wait only for those in view while the rest live on and think less often. Pausing people outright would put them out of step with the seasons and with neighbours who walk in and out of view, and would make every run depend on where the watcher looked, so that two runs could no longer be compared. If the lighter choice proves not enough in a real run, this is where to look next.
- **A pen for a herd, and herds that are driven:** a place at a settlement where kept animals stay and breed when their keeper walks away, and a way to drive a wild herd toward it. (Generation 22 made a herd follow its lead when one of it is tamed; it still lives where its keeper lives.)
- **A settlement that feeds itself as a settlement:** fields, a fishing place and a larder that belong to those who live there, so that a settlement is better at food than its members are alone. To be designed from what the next run shows of stores and rich ground.
- **Flood in the season of rains:** rivers that rise, and floods that take what is built too near them. (The season of rains itself was built in Generation 21; flood changes the ground under buildings and paths and waits to see the rains run first.)
- **A day that goes round the world,** so that the sun lights one side of the globe and it is morning in one country while it is evening in another.
- **The ice and the rainforest as places to live:** more of what Nova named for them as beings reach them (more craft from hide and bone, fishing through ice, tree-dwellings), from what real runs show is missing.
- **Sea creatures that return** from the open sea when their numbers fall, as land animals do from the rim.

**From Nova's notes of 22 November, afternoon, in the order proposed:**

- **Grudges and their healing** (the grudge itself, from a blow or a theft, and its mending by gifts: built in 18). Still to build: hatred that arises from defeat or a stolen mate and lasts until healed or paid for with large compensation; other hard feelings flare and die down fast. **Peacemakers and peace ceremonies.** All of it rarer inside a family or band than across them, and arising from conflict, great differences in wealth, history, or charisma.
- **More of the drama:** jealousy of what others have, self-sacrifice for family or tribe, pride and over-confidence that help or harm, greed, and beside them kindness, generosity and charity. (Many of these already press on beings as drives; what is missing is acts and consequences that follow from them.)
- **Rivalry for mates:** competition, mates won away or defecting, a child at once with the one who wins them.
- **Heroes.** Standing and deeds that spread as lore; songs and poems about them; tribute from those who meet them; challenges and duels from the ambitious. The winner of a fight or duel takes four fifths of the loser's wealth and may take their mate.
- **Fighting together** (hunting and standing against beasts together: built in 18 as something anyone can do and grows better at). Still to build: these as ways in the tree with their own discovery; fighting other people as a group; calling for help and answering it as things that are learned.
- **A veiled rim for a world made before Generation 17,** so that Nova's long world can grow: land made beyond its edges that continues its rivers and shores, and every map re-indexed, tested on saved worlds.
- **Talents and quests.** Skill in one way raising the pull and the chance of the next along its line (a first step exists: knowing neighbouring ways lowers what the next one takes). Learning what a way demands through dreams as skill grows, visions from certain herbs, or a shaman's prophecy; quests to gather the ways, practice and things it needs.
- **Bandits** in new lands; stranger settlements that are plainly friendly or hostile, with things to trade.
- **Herding and farming as a settlement's steady food:** farms, flocks kept and bred.

- **Objects of culture** beyond the first talismans and carvings: special gems, named relics that gather a history. **Secret manuscripts** holding spells, knowledge or cures.
- **Religious figures, sages with followers, cults, schools of thought and religions** growing out of teachings, that form and compete, some of the light and some of the dark. Benefits, secrets, abilities and costs for those who belong, in some cases sacrifice. Shrines, temples and churches that members pay toward.
- **Tribute and taxes** for the common good: stores, works, defence. **Guilds and secret societies** that give benefit and take dues.
- **Schools** for children and the young, later universities: skills learned faster than alone.
- **Trade between lands and across water; ports and docks; colonies** as a named act. **Diplomats, spies, saboteurs; alliances** open and secret.
- **The world growing at its edges** when a people crosses the sea or reaches a far shore by ship: a whole new land, with its own kind, riches and perhaps people. (Needs every map array re-indexed when the grid grows, tested on saved worlds.)
- **Kinds of rule:** councils and votes, shared holding, private holding, rule by the armed.
- **Later roles:** alchemists, scholars, scientists, as ways and standing allow. (The names exist; little yet depends on them.)

## LATER: ages three and four

- **Monsters.** Dragons and great beasts that wander in rarely, attack, and can be killed only by a band acting together; slaying one gives much food and boons. With high skill they can be captured, tamed and bred as weapons of war or defence. They breed from eggs without mating, need a great deal of food, and the eggs hatch slowly, must be guarded, can be stolen and stolen back. Everyone wants them: those who learn of them organise to steal them, and their owners to defend them.

- **Siege warfare and defensive works;** weapons of war carried to a siege.
- **Upkeep.** What is built must be kept up or it decays. Old buildings taken down to raise new ones (taking down is built). Concrete and metal as building stuffs, mined and carried from where they are won.
- **The troubles of crowding, and their remedies.** Religion, care for the poor, clean water, public works, places for play, music, dance and art, the handling of waste and its recycling. Places of buying and selling. Healers, pharmacies, hospitals.
- **Places of invention** that quicken discovery. **Revolutions in knowledge** that give great advantage to those who take them up, while others hold out or follow.
- **The sciences as knowledge in their own right, in generations:** biology, chemistry, physics, then the second and third of each, beneath the technologies that need them. (The ways exist in the tree; the discoveries have no mechanics yet.)

## FAR: ages five and six

- **Sources of power, in order:** coal, oil and gas, falling water, wind, the atom, the sun, fusion, the ground of things. Each needs what must be found: oil fields, uranium, copper, silicon, rare earths, carbon, diamond. (Oil, uranium and diamond are in the ground; the rest are not.)
- **Speech at a distance, in order:** marks, smoke, birds, radio, telephone, the joined machines, the machine in the hand. Reckoning machines need factories to make them.
- **Machines that fly:** balloons, propellers, jets, for carrying and for war. Land vehicles for war.
- **The buildings of a great city:** dwellings for many, high towers, offices, halls of rule, guards and fire-watch, water and power works and the lines that carry power, barracks and camps of war, markets, harbours, airfields, stations, factories, plants that recycle.
- **The knowledge of what makes a body:** changing animals and oneself.
- **Weapons beyond guns.**
- **New materials far down the tree:** plastics, silicon, rare earths.

## LAST

- **A spaceport.** Ships that come and go, goods and strange devices from other worlds, visitors who need their own air and live beside the port. Their devices do what nothing else does (power, food, healing, terrible weapons, things not understood) and are worth much for being rare.
- **Visitors with an agenda of their own,** who may be taken for gods until the beings learn what they are.

## Standing themes, in no fixed order

These run through every stage and are picked up as runs call for them.

- Tribal bonds and family bonds, and the pull between them; mating within a people and between peoples; speciation over generations.
- More kinds of animals at several levels of predator and prey; working animals; eggs.
- Many herbs and plants that heal, harm, change attributes or behaviour, or open the mind; combinations that can be learned.
- Spirits of places, good and ill, rarely found; mythical animals, monsters and places.
- Shamans who heal or affect crops, weather, animals and people; celebrations and ceremonies; crime and punishment; secret knowledge and shared knowledge.
- Professions and castes that develop and can be taught; promises and debts; rituals with force.
- Wealth, hoarding and raiding; trade between groups; currency.
- Beings inventing a kind of building, tool or ceremony themselves, not only choosing from what exists.
- A world rich to look at and to hear that changes as it ages: cities that visibly grow, roads that join settlements, music that marks events; all of it open to the maker.
- Hills as a terrain; fleets and battles at sea.

## Known open problems

- **Starvation in winter.** Ten of seventeen over 41 days. Generation 15 brought storage, but on the eve of famine; Generation 16 answers with foresight in autumn, a land that no longer runs down, a milder winter and bodies that reach their stores.
- **Peoples too small and too closely related to grow.** Generation 16 answered with newcomers, twins and longer lives, and the world went from six to twelve; Generation 17 adds women and men, which narrows who can pair, and bands of four to offset it.
- **A carried-on world cannot grow at its edges.** Only a new world has the veiled rim. Now in NEXT.
- **Nothing in Generation 20 has been balanced by a run:** new hunters (wolves, bears, snakes, shadowcats, rivermaws), new food, three new peoples.
- **Prompts are now the first thing to cut:** middle 30,700 characters and longest 45,600 in the fourteenth run, with forty-three alive. Refusals began at this size. Generation 23 changed the pace only, so that the next run shows what the pace alone does; cut the prompt next.
- **Prompts grew through Generation 21 too:** median 29,000 characters and longest 38,000 in the twelfth run (24,000 and 36,800 in the eighth). No call failed and a thought took 7 seconds, so nothing was cut in Generation 21 beyond the list of plants; if thoughts slow or fail, this is the first place to look.
- **Not yet used by real minds in twelve days on the globe:** fishing, the canoe, kayak, sled and igloo; one animal tamed. Too soon to call a fault.
- **A band of one people that settles in another's climate** (Wood-folk on the ice) pays for it heavily in winter. The pull of the homeland did not bring them back. Watch before changing anything.
- **A thought took 10 seconds in the eleventh run,** three times what it took in the tenth. Unexplained; it sets how often a being can think.
- **Acts that never reached a body.** `claim` and `venture` were unreachable for real minds in Generations 16 and 17 because tests called the body directly. Fixed in 18, with a check that reads the acts out of the prompt.
- **Minds hunt very little:** 2 plan steps of 1,274 in the tenth run. Generation 18 points the hungry at prey; unknown whether they will go.
- **Speed.** A world moment costs about 2.8 ms in the larger world, up from 2.
- **Nothing passes between strangers.** In seven runs: no trade, no planned fight, no animal tamed, and peoples that meet a handful of times.
- **No tale, song, myth or teaching has yet been made** by a real mind, though sayings and hard lessons spread and cross between peoples.
- **The record of a run is too large to read from the store.** An interrupted run can be read only through its final state and, from Generation 15, the maker's own small document. A full analysis needs the downloaded run log.
- **The later ages are sketches.** From about the fourth age the ways, minerals and buildings have names and looks but little that they do.
- **Prompts are long and growing:** a middle of 24,000 characters and a longest of 36,800 in the eighth run.
- **The maker's report that recipe counts did not update** in the sixth run: no fault found.
