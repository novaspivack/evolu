# Groups: a proposal for discussion

Written by Claude for Nova Spivack, 24 November 2026, after Generation 32. **Nothing here is built.** You asked for the thinking first and a decision afterwards. This page puts one question to you, with the options, the answer I propose and why, and then the smaller questions that follow from it.

## The problem, stated with the numbers we have

- Every being in Evolu is a mind, and a mind is a call to Claude. The page is allowed about four calls at once. In the fourteenth run, 43 alive with prompts of 30,000 characters brought refusals, and the minds slept eighteen days of the world before anyone saw. So a world holds about forty thinking beings, whatever its age.
- A world begun in the fifth age now has a town of about 110 buildings and 15 people. It looks like a town and is peopled like a camp. The troubles you listed for later ages (unrest, poverty, markets, crime, rule) are things crowds do. Fifteen people cannot have them.
- The bodies are cheap beside the minds (about 3 ms a moment for fifteen to forty), but not free: cost rises with the number alive.

So the real constraint is this: **minds are scarce, and population is not allowed to grow past them.** Groups are the question of what to do about that.

## The question

**When there are more people than can each have a mind, what is the unit that thinks, and where does the story live?**

## The options

| | What it is | What it keeps | What it costs or loses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Do not build it** | Worlds stay at about forty minds. Later ages are small places with advanced works. | Everything Evolu is now. No new machinery. | Cities stay hollow. The troubles of later ages have nobody to happen to. |
| **B. A crowd as scenery** | The minds stay as they are. Unnamed residents walk the streets, drawn but not counted. | Simple. The town looks alive. | The crowd does nothing and wants nothing. This is the version most likely to be boring: it is decoration. |
| **C. The place as one mind** | Past a size, a settlement becomes one mind (a council). It reads figures and sets policy. The people are statistics. | Scales to any population on one call a day. | The individual stories, which are what Evolu has that a city game does not. It becomes a policy game with a narrator. |
| **D. Three layers** | Persons, the many, and groups that speak for the many. Described below. | The individual stories stay at the centre, and the stakes become those of a society. | The most machinery: a second way of counting people that must obey the same laws as bodies. |

## What I propose: D, in stages, with the first stage as a test of whether to go on

**Persons.** Full minds, exactly as now, about forty in the world. In a city they are the people the story follows: those who lead, and a few others whom events have singled out.

**The many.** Everyone else lives in households attached to dwellings. A household is a few numbers: how many, how fed, how well off, what work (from the works it is attached to), which people and tradition, how it feels. Households are stepped once a day by the same laws that govern bodies: they eat from real stores, they are born and die at the rates bodies are, their work yields what a tended work yields. On the map they are seen as small figures going between home, work and market along the streets. Those figures are a picture of the household's day, not separate things to simulate.

**Groups.** The many do not think one by one, and the city does not think as one. Households that share a trade, a quarter, a tradition or a people form a group: the weavers, the river quarter, the congregation of the Open Hand. **A group is a mind.** It thinks once a day, or when something weighty happens to it. It speaks as "we" in Voices. It keeps a journal as a person does. It can ask, give, withhold work, pledge itself to a person, oppose one, muster. A city has perhaps three to six. That is three to six calls a day for hundreds of people.

**Rising and returning.** A member of the many becomes a person when something singles them out: chosen to lead a group, wronged in a way that is chronicled, married to a person, or picked by you with a "follow this one" control. They get a mind, a name and a past written from their household's record. A person who grows old and obscure can return to the many, and their memory stays as a tale their household holds. The number of minds stays about the same; who holds them changes.

**This starts with population, not with age.** When a birth would take the world past its minds, the child joins a household instead. A fishing clan of sixty at a river mouth in the first age would have a group, exactly as you said it might.

### How a city and a small community live in one world

The small community stays as it is: all persons. They meet the city through things that can be seen and walked up to.

- **Trade:** a party from the city (a person, or a few bodies sent by a group) comes down the road with goods in a wagon. This is the caravans item already on the roadmap, and Generation 32's vehicles are what it would use.
- **Leaving and arriving:** a villager who walks into the city may stay ("went to the city": a person joining the many). A household that leaves the city arrives at the village as newcomers do now.
- **Force:** a group musters a levy, bodies on instinct led by a person. A village sees fifty coming up the road.
- **Land:** the city's fields and its felling spread as its households grow. That pressure reaches the village before any person does.
- **What a villager perceives:** not statistics. "A crowd of about four hundred. A market. The hall of the weavers, where many are gathered and angry."

### Why this and not the others

- It is the only option in which population can grow and the individual stories are still the centre. In A the later ages stay empty. C trades away what is distinctive. B is the first stage of D without the part that gives the crowd a will.
- The troubles you want later become **data on groups** and not new code each time: unrest is a group's mood past a law; a strike is a group withholding its work; poverty is households unfed beside a full granary; rule is which groups have pledged to whom. That fits the rule that new content be data.
- It keeps calls nearly flat.

### What could make it bad, and what I would watch

- **Two kinds of people that do not agree.** If households eat or breed at different rates from bodies, the world cheats. The check: run the same village once as bodies and once as households and compare food, births and deaths over forty days on ten seeds.
- **Groups that talk like committees.** A group's mind must be told only what its members could see and feel, and asked what it wants. If the first real run gives minutes of meetings, the design is wrong and should stop there.
- **The map is small for a city.** One tile is one building. A city of four hundred needs either a wide footprint or blocks drawn in more detail than a tile. I would lay cities out in districts by rule (the street plan of Generation 32 is the beginning of that) and test how many fit before promising a number.
- **Rising and returning could feel arbitrary.** The rule for who is singled out must be visible in the chronicle.

## The stages, and where you could stop

1. **The many** (households, counted and fed; figures on the streets; births past the cap join a household). No group minds yet. This shows whether a town of two hundred runs at speed, eats correctly and looks alive. **It is the test of whether this is worth doing.** If it is dull to watch, stop: little is lost.
2. **Groups that speak** (formed from households; think daily; ask, withhold, pledge, muster; levers and figures for the maker).
3. **Rising and returning**, with "follow this one".
4. **Cities among villages** (caravans, leaving and arriving, levies, the spreading footprint, districts).

Then the troubles of later ages, as data on groups.

## The smaller questions that are yours

1. **Where does the story live?** Options: with persons, groups as the chorus; or with the place. Proposed: persons, with the chronicler told to write both. Why: it is what Evolu already does well.
2. **Who decides who has a mind?** Options: the world, by what happens to people; you; both. Proposed: both. Why: the world keeps it honest, and you will want to follow someone you noticed.
3. **Are the many counted or scenery?** Proposed: counted, under the same laws. Why: otherwise nothing a group says is about anything.
4. **How large?** Options: about 200 in a city, or thousands. Proposed: aim at 200 to 400 first, one figure on the street for each person, and decide after seeing it. Why: past a few hundred the map and the picture both need a second design.
5. **Build stage 1 as an experiment before deciding the rest?** Proposed: yes.

## What I do not know

How real minds will treat a crowd, and whether a group's voice will read as a people or as a committee. No test of mine can show either. Only a run can.
